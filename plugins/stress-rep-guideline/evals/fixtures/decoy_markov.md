# Cost-effectiveness of Drug X versus standard care for chronic condition Y: a Markov cohort model

F. Fictitious and G. Madeup — Centre for Imaginary Health Economics (fictional)

## 1. Objective

To estimate the incremental cost per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) of Drug X compared with standard care for adults with chronic condition Y from the perspective of a national health service, over a lifetime horizon.

## 2. Model structure

We developed a cohort Markov model with four mutually exclusive health states: *stable disease*, *progressed disease*, *severe disease* and *death*. A hypothetical cohort of 1,000 patients aged 55 enters the model in the stable state. The cycle length is one year with a half-cycle correction. Transitions between states are governed by an annual transition probability matrix (Table 1); death is absorbing and includes background mortality from national life tables.

| From \ To | Stable | Progressed | Severe | Death |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stable (standard care) | 0.82 | 0.12 | 0.03 | 0.03 |
| Stable (Drug X) | 0.88 | 0.08 | 0.01 | 0.03 |
| Progressed | 0.00 | 0.80 | 0.14 | 0.06 |
| Severe | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.85 | 0.15 |

Drug X acts by lowering the probability of progression from the stable state; its relative risk of 0.67 was taken from the pivotal trial (Fictitious et al., 2022, n = 840).

## 3. Costs and utilities

Annual costs (2023 prices) and utility weights per health state are listed in Table 2. Costs and QALYs are discounted at 3.5 % per year.

| State | Annual cost (standard care) | Annual cost (Drug X) | Utility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stable | 2,100 | 6,900 | 0.85 |
| Progressed | 5,400 | 10,200 | 0.68 |
| Severe | 14,800 | 19,600 | 0.42 |

## 4. Analysis

Deterministic results are reported as incremental costs, incremental QALYs and the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). Parameter uncertainty was explored with a probabilistic sensitivity analysis of 5,000 Monte Carlo draws (beta distributions for probabilities and utilities, gamma distributions for costs) and summarised as a cost-effectiveness acceptability curve. One-way sensitivity analyses are presented as a tornado diagram.

## 5. Results

Drug X produced 0.74 additional QALYs at an additional cost of 21,300, giving an ICER of 28,800 per QALY. At a willingness-to-pay threshold of 30,000 per QALY, Drug X was cost-effective in 56 % of probabilistic draws.

## 6. Conclusion

Drug X is likely to be cost-effective at conventional thresholds, with substantial decision uncertainty driven by the progression probability.
